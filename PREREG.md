## The claim under test
Nitschke, Krapp, Cathcart, Gallot, Bosshard, Burkart, Berthet, Townsend, Surbeck, Zuberbuehler &
Bickel (2026), "Supra-regular recursion in primate vocal sequences", bioRxiv
[10.64898/2026.09.19.752870](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.19.752870v1)
(v1, 21 Sep 2026; covered by ScienceAlert). Their abstract: the call sequences of bonobos,
olive colobus monkeys and common marmosets "include parts that are most likely generated by
supra-regular recursive grammars, similar to human language", which "challenge[s] syntax as
the major transition in hominid evolution".

Their method (Materials and Methods, Text S1-S3): in 20-fold cross-validation, an n-gram model
(orders 1-6, modified Kneser-Ney via KenLM; their 'pREG') and a fully connected Chomsky-normal-
form PCFG (6-20 non-terminals, Dirichlet prior, 600 epochs; their 'pCFG') are fitted to the
training folds; ELLD per fold = mean held-out log-likelihood difference pCFG minus pREG. Result:
in each species "about as many folds" have ELLD > 0 as ELLD < 0, read as "parts of the data
[that] are best captured by hierarchical structures that require supra-regular mechanisms".
Their false-positive check (Text S3, Fig. S3) simulated only n-gram generators (orders 2-5).
The theory paper (Krapp & Nitschke 2026, arXiv:2609.23567) shows that fully connected PCFG
distributions are disjoint from n-gram distributions. Data and code: "available on
publication" (not public on 2026-11-25).

## Why it may not show what it says (the gaps we test)
1. **Regular is not n-gram.** Probabilistic regular grammars (right-linear rules) are hidden
   Markov models / probabilistic finite automata, a strictly larger class than n-grams. A PCFG
   can represent every HMM exactly (Tsushima et al. 2017; our `pcfg_from_hmm`, unit-tested).
   So a PCFG beating n-grams on held-out data is expected for any regular source that is not
   an n-gram, and the disjointness theorem says nothing about such sources.
2. **Finite data are always regular.** In the public marmoset data no sequence is longer than
   9 calls (73% are single calls; see 'already seen' below). Any probability distribution over
   a finite set of sequences is generated by a probabilistic finite automaton. Likelihood
   comparisons on such data can only show which model class generalises better, not that the
   source needs recursion.
3. **Fold signs are noise, not 'parts'.** CV folds are random, exchangeable partitions. If the
   two models predict about equally well, each fold's sign is a coin flip, and "about half the
   folds" is what one expects with no supra-regular part at all.

## Pipeline (identical for the real data and every simulated world)
- 20 random folds (seeded). In each fold, fit on the other 19, score each held-out sequence.
- n-gram ('pREG' as published): order 1 = maximum-likelihood unigram with end token; orders 2-6
  = interpolated modified Kneser-Ney with start pads and end token (our implementation of the
  KenLM estimator; unit-tested to sum to 1).
- HMM (a true probabilistic regular grammar): K in {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8}, Baum-Welch, pseudo-count
  0.01, at most 300 EM steps (relative tolerance 1e-6), one seeded start.
- PCFG (fully connected CNF, start symbol 0): primary grid N in {6, 12} non-terminals,
  pseudo-count 0.01 on every rule (MAP EM, keeps every rule non-zero), at most 300 EM steps.
  Real data only, secondary: N = 20 and pseudo-count 0.1 for N = 6 and 12.
- Selection A (primary): per model class, the setting with the highest total held-out
  log-likelihood over all folds (as they chose n-gram order and non-terminal count).
  Selection B (secondary, the literal formula of their ELLD with a per-sequence max): per
  held-out sequence, the max over settings within each class.
- Per comparison: fold ELLD (mean per-sequence difference), **F = share of the 20 folds with
  ELLD > 0**, F_noise (share expected if folds were redrawn at random from the same
  per-sequence differences, 200 redraws), fold-level t test of mean ELLD = 0, Cochran's Q for
  between-fold heterogeneity (Wilson-Hilferty p).

## Tests
- **T0 (reproduce):** coding gives n = 5800, c = 6 as in their Table S1; real F (pCFG vs best
  n-gram, selection A) lies in [0.25, 0.75] ("about as many folds").
- **T1 (PRIMARY, calibration):** simulated worlds of 5800 sequences (max length 12, longer draws
  rejected), each run through the identical pipeline. Families:
  - **B, bootstrap**: resample the real coded sequences with replacement. A finite
    distribution, regular by construction. (Conservative: held-out sequences are more often
    also in training than in real data, which favours memorising n-grams.)
  - **H, HMM**: the HMM fitted to all real sequences, K in 1-8 chosen by BIC (log of the number
    of sequences). Regular by construction, not an n-gram.
  - **G, n-gram control**: KN trigram fitted to the real data (the kind of source their Text S3
    checked). Expected to give F near 0; if not, our pipeline differs from theirs.
  - **P, PCFG power**: the N = 6, pseudo 0.01 PCFG fitted to all real data. Genuinely
    context-free source.
  Two jobs (seed offsets 0 and 100000), up to 30 worlds per family each, families run in
  round-robin order until a 320-minute budget; graded on all completed worlds pooled. A family
  counts if it has at least 8 worlds.
- **T2 (heterogeneity, real):** Cochran's Q of the 20 fold ELLDs (pCFG vs n-gram).
- **T3 (regular baseline, real):** pCFG vs best HMM: mean ELLD, F, fold t test.
- **T4 (selection B):** F under the per-sequence max, real and worlds.

## Headline rule (computed from F = pCFG vs best n-gram, selection A)
- **NOT_DIAGNOSTIC**: at least one counted regular family (B or H) has median F >= 0.25. The
  published fold pattern is what regular sources produce at this sample size; folds favouring
  the pCFG are not evidence of supra-regular parts in the marmoset data.
- **DIAGNOSTIC**: both B and H counted, both with 95th percentile F <= 0.15. Then the claim's
  support depends on the real F and on T3.
- **PARTIAL**: anything else. **INSUFFICIENT_WORLDS** if neither B nor H reaches 8 worlds.
- Flag **MODEL_UNCALIBRATED** if the control family G has median F > 0.25 (our pipeline would
  then also flag n-gram sources, unlike theirs); the headline is then reported with that flag
  and no claim about their exact implementation is made.
- T3 reading: PCFG_BEYOND_HMM if mean ELLD vs HMM > 0 with fold t-test p < 0.05;
  HMM_MATCHES_OR_BEATS if mean ELLD vs HMM <= 0; otherwise UNDECIDED.

## Limits fixed in advance
Our estimators are not their code (KenLM vs our KN; MAP EM vs their Bayesian induction; fewer
epochs and a smaller non-terminal grid). The control family G and the real-data T0 check show
how close our pipeline behaves. Only the marmoset data are public; nothing here speaks to the
bonobo or colobus data except through the logic of gaps 1-3. A NOT_DIAGNOSTIC headline does not
show that marmoset calls are regular; it shows that this test cannot tell.
